- Far tree levels no longer pop darker than level 0 when a tree switches level (issue #432). Four changes:
  - The standing card of each clump on levels 2 and 3 is rolled by the golden angle from the last clump. All of them used to face one world direction, so a far tree drew about three times darker from some sides than from others (0.80 of level 0 from one side and 0.27 from the others, for a conifer under even sky light).
  - The clump cards of levels 2 and 3 take their own normals again instead of blending 70% toward the crown's (`clumpCanopyBlend`, 0 by default, 0.3 for the bush). With the crown normal, a sun behind a tree left its far levels near black while level 0 still glowed through its leaves.
  - The level match in `src/tree-sky.js` takes the mean sky visibility over the leaf each vertex stands for (`vertexCoverage`, a third of the area of its triangles) instead of over the vertices, because a clump has few vertices for a lot of leaf.
  - Each species carries a colour gain and a sky gain for levels 2 and 3 in `src/tree-level-gains.js`. `node scripts/measure-tree-level-luminance.mjs --fit` solves them: it draws each level against level 0 from three sides under sky light, a sun behind and a sun in front, and brings the ratios close to 1. The file holds the geometric mean of the fits on seeds 1 to 6, recentred a little below 1. Trees of one species still differ, so each tree's colour gain also follows how much clump area its far level carries against level 0 (`area`, `areaExponent`). Over seeds 1 to 8, each species' mean ratio of levels 2 and 3 to level 0 lies within 0.90 to 1.07 under every light (0.15 to 0.66 on main for the four trees), and 226 of the 240 seed ratios (5 species, 2 levels, 3 lights, 8 seeds) land within 0.85 to 1.15.
  - The probe (`scripts/measure-tree-level-luminance.mjs`) also prints the ratio from each side (`sides`), which is how the one-way cards showed up: pooled over three sides, they averaged out.
- `tests/tree-sky-levels.test.js` pins the coverage-weighted mean and the range of the gains. This changes how far trees look.
