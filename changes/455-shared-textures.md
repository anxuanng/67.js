- A texture that both parts of a split showcase world use is now stored, downloaded and decoded once (part of #455). `scripts/split-showcase-glb.mjs` keeps such a texture in the first part only; the rest holds a 1x1 stand-in image and the same key in the texture's `extras.sharedTexture`. `loadGltf` and `parseGltf` take `options.sharedTextures` (made by the new `createSharedTextures()`): a texture with a key already decoded by an earlier file is reused, so the parts of all files bind the same texture descriptors and the renderer uploads and counts each once. The split script also warns when an image still sits in both parts, and writes the draw-order ranks as runs (`partRankRuns`, about 9 KB less JSON in the airport rest).
- Measured in headless Chrome on a local `vite preview` build, cache off, 5 Mbps (`scripts/measure-showcase-load.mjs`). Airport: 18.87 to 18.15 MB for the whole world (one-piece GLB: 18.10 MB), texture memory 196 to 161 MiB (one-piece: 161 MiB), first frame 4.2-4.5 s (was 4.5 s), final frame 27.4-28.2 s (was 29.4 s). Port (13.43 MB, 447 MiB) and datacenter (14.08 MB, 196 MiB) share no texture between parts and hold the same. The final frame of all three worlds is byte-identical to main at the same camera (maximum difference 0).
- The 0.05 MB the airport still sends over the one-piece GLB is the split's own cost: the 33 KB first-frame sky preview and 8 KB of JSON that both parts carry. No texture travels twice.
