- A terrain that does not set `mirrorLevels` now builds its coarse water-mirror meshes (up to two halvings the resolution allows) when `applyWaterReflection` finds a water surface in the scene, so an agent needs no flag (part of #316). The levels are built from the heights the terrain already keeps. A terrain in a scene without water builds none of them, and an explicit `mirrorLevels`, 0 included, is kept as given. On the 900 m benchmark terrain the one-time build takes about 130 ms inside `applyWaterReflection`, adds 22 MB of mesh data, and leaves terrain without water at its old memory and build time. With no `mirrorLevels` in the benchmark call the mirror frame costs +25% over reflection off (8.08 against 6.45 ms, 240 samples per variant, RTX 3060), and the picture is pixel for pixel the one made with `mirrorLevels: 2`. The first frame after attaching takes 5.7 ms of GPU time, a normal frame.
- `scripts/benchmark-water-reflection.mjs` leaves `mirrorLevels` unset unless `--mirror-levels N` is given, and reports the `applyWaterReflection` time, the mirror mesh size and the first frame after attach (part of #316).
